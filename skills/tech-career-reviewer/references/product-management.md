# Product management evaluation

Apply this reference to product manager, product owner, technical product manager, growth product manager, and product leadership targets.

## Strong signals

- Clear problem framing grounded in user, market, operational, or business evidence.
- Decisions and prioritization under constraints, including what was not built and why.
- Outcomes separated from feature shipment.
- Discovery, experimentation, and learning that changed a decision.
- Cross-functional leadership with explicit influence and decision rights.
- Metrics with baselines, guardrails, timeframes, and the candidate's contribution.
- Technical or domain depth appropriate to the target role.

Roadmap size and number of launches are supporting context, not proof of product judgment.

## Seniority calibration

- **Associate or junior:** supports discovery and delivery, analyzes evidence, communicates clearly, and owns scoped product work.
- **Mid-level:** independently owns a problem area, prioritizes tradeoffs, aligns a team, and delivers measurable learning or outcomes.
- **Senior:** leads ambiguous strategy, connects multiple teams, shapes investment choices, and produces durable product outcomes.
- **Lead, principal, or head:** sets portfolio direction, creates organizational mechanisms, develops product talent, and influences company-level results.

## Common weak signals

- Lists of features, ceremonies, and stakeholders with no decisions or outcomes.
- Revenue or growth claims that imply sole ownership of a cross-functional result.
- "Led" without specifying decision authority or mechanism of influence.
- Discovery mentioned without what was learned or changed.
- Metrics reported without baseline, period, or guardrails.

## Evidence questions

1. What user or business problem were you accountable for?
2. What evidence changed your understanding or decision?
3. What alternatives did you reject, delay, or de-scope?
4. Which metric moved, over what period, and against what baseline?
5. What guardrail prevented a locally positive but harmful outcome?
6. What decision did you personally own versus influence?
7. How did engineering, design, data, operations, or commercial partners shape the result?

## Proof recommendations

Prefer a sanitized product case study containing problem framing, evidence, options, decision, tradeoffs, launch or experiment, outcome, and learning. A concise strategy memo or metric tree can also demonstrate judgment without exposing confidential roadmap data.
