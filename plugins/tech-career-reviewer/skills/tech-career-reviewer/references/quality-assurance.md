# Quality assurance evaluation

Apply this reference to manual QA, automation, software development engineer in test, performance testing, quality engineering, and test leadership targets.

## Strong signals

- Risk-based test strategy connected to product and technical failure modes.
- Automation choices explained by coverage value, maintenance cost, execution time, and feedback speed.
- Quality outcomes such as escaped-defect reduction, earlier detection, release confidence, flaky-test reduction, or faster regression cycles.
- Integration with CI/CD, observability, environments, test data, and developer workflows.
- Clear ownership across prevention, detection, diagnosis, and continuous improvement.
- Product judgment: knowing what not to automate and where exploratory testing adds value.
- Influence on requirements, acceptance criteria, architecture testability, and team quality practices.

Raw test-case counts and coverage percentages require context; more is not automatically better.

## Seniority calibration

- **Entry or junior:** executes tests carefully, reports actionable defects, understands basic risk, and contributes maintainable cases.
- **Mid-level:** owns feature quality, builds reliable automation, improves feedback loops, and collaborates early in delivery.
- **Senior:** defines cross-feature strategy, tackles systemic quality risks, improves test architecture, and influences engineering practices.
- **Lead or principal:** creates organization-wide quality mechanisms, standards, platforms, and measurable risk reduction.

## Common weak signals

- Long tool lists with no quality outcome.
- "Created test cases" without risk, coverage intent, or defects prevented.
- Automation volume presented without flakiness or maintenance context.
- QA portrayed only as a final release gate.
- Coverage claims without defining layer, scope, or usefulness.

## Evidence questions

1. Which product or technical risks did you prioritize, and why?
2. What defects or failure patterns were escaping before your change?
3. What did you automate, and what did you intentionally leave manual?
4. How did execution time, flakiness, detection time, or release confidence change?
5. How was the test suite integrated into CI/CD and developer feedback?
6. How did you manage environments, test data, or observability?
7. What quality decision did you influence outside the QA function?

## Proof recommendations

Prefer a sanitized test strategy, risk matrix, automation architecture, CI quality gate, exploratory testing charter, defect-learning narrative, or example of a high-quality bug report. Never expose customer or employer secrets.
