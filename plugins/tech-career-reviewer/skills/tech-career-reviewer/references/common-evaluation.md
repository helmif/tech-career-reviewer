# Common evaluation

Use this rubric for every supported role. Score only dimensions that have enough evidence.

## Score scale

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0 | Contradictory, misleading, or absent where essential |
| 1 | Weak signal; mostly generic assertions or duties |
| 2 | Plausible signal; some specific evidence but important gaps remain |
| 3 | Strong signal; clear, relevant, credible evidence |
| 4 | Exceptional signal; differentiated evidence with scope, outcomes, and proof |

Scores describe the supplied materials, not the candidate's inherent ability. Do not calculate an overall score when more than two dimensions lack evidence.

## Core dimensions

| Dimension | Weight | Evaluate |
|---|---:|---|
| Target positioning | 15 | The intended role, domain, level, and value proposition are quickly understandable. |
| Outcomes and impact | 20 | Work connects to measurable or otherwise verifiable change. |
| Ownership and scope | 15 | Personal contribution, decision authority, collaborators, scale, and constraints are clear. |
| Role-specific depth | 15 | Evidence demonstrates the craft signals in the selected role reference. |
| Target relevance | 15 | Strongest evidence maps to the actual target rather than listing every past activity. |
| Credibility and consistency | 10 | Titles, dates, skills, claims, and seniority signals align across sources. |
| Scanability and discoverability | 5 | Structure, terminology, and section hierarchy support fast human and machine reading. |
| External proof | 5 | Portfolios, repositories, artifacts, recommendations, or public work support key claims. |

If the user requests a composite score, calculate the weighted average of scored dimensions and disclose which dimensions were excluded. Never label it an ATS score or hiring probability.

## Seniority calibration

Calibrate evidence to the target level rather than years of experience alone.

- **Entry or junior:** foundations, learning velocity, reliable execution, collaboration, and completed work.
- **Mid-level:** independent delivery, sound decisions, end-to-end ownership, and repeatable outcomes.
- **Senior:** ambiguity reduction, complex tradeoffs, cross-functional influence, system or product outcomes, and mentoring.
- **Staff, lead, principal, or head:** multi-team leverage, strategy, durable mechanisms, organization-wide influence, and outcomes beyond one project.

Do not penalize candidates for lacking higher-level signals when those signals are not required by the target.

## Channel checks

### CV or resume

- Confirm the first third establishes target fit.
- Prefer a conventional single-column reading order when ATS compatibility matters.
- Check headings, dates, role hierarchy, bullet density, repetition, and unexplained acronyms.
- Surface the strongest relevant evidence before lower-value duties.
- Flag unsupported proficiency labels and exhaustive tool inventories.

### LinkedIn

- Check whether the headline, About section, experience, skills, featured work, and target role tell the same story.
- Write for both recruiter search terminology and human credibility.
- Avoid copying the CV verbatim when a concise narrative or featured proof would add value.
- Treat completeness as useful only when the content supports the target.

### Job match

Classify each important requirement as:

- **Demonstrated:** directly supported by evidence.
- **Transferable:** adjacent evidence plausibly transfers; explain the bridge.
- **Unverified:** candidate may have it, but current materials do not establish it.
- **Missing:** candidate has not claimed it.
- **Irrelevant or inflated:** not materially required despite appearing in the posting.

Separate hard requirements from preferences. Do not recommend dishonest keyword insertion.

## Fairness and relevance

Ignore age, sex, gender identity, ethnicity, religion, disability, marital or family status, photographs, and other protected or sensitive characteristics when assessing professional fit. Location, language, or work authorization may be discussed only when the candidate or job requirement makes it materially relevant; present it as a logistical constraint, not a quality signal.
